# SAOR Theory at a Glance v1.3.0

SAOR studies when two arithmetic realizations with identical visible sum and product remain distinguishable after the same arithmetic context is appended.

The theory proceeds through three levels.

## Formal origin resolution

`MASKED` means exact twinhood in both retained-factor transfer graphs. `UNMASKED` has a concrete one-sided-neighbour certificate.

## General synthesis

For equal-projection base pairs satisfying Prime Cover and Atom Deficit—and, by the Anchored-Cone Corollary, for every distinct pair once an explicit finite anchor condition is met—:

`product side -> automatically twin`

`sum side -> exact safe-filler problem`

`large residuals -> two-filler separator`

`all exceptions -> finite low-residual table`

`context dependence -> N, S + finitely many low-prime support bits`.

This produces an effective exact masking classifier and a future-stable finite signature.

## Sharp SAOR classifier

For the fixed two-module system, the complete future signature is

`K(E)=(D4,D5,eps5,eps7,eps11)`.

The support set `{5,7,11}` is the unique minimum among classifiers of the form `(N,S,supp intersect C)`, and unequal signatures admit constructive future separators.

The aggregate rank-two affine compression is retained as a structural corollary: it describes precisely the aggregate `(N,S)` part of the richer support-sensitive theory.

## Note on dual twinhood

Under Prime Cover and Atom Deficit the product side is always twin, so `MASKED` is decided by the sum side alone. The product side can unmask only below the atom threshold or when an uncovered prime is absent.
